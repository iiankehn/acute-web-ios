# Acute Web for iOS and iPadOS — Privacy Policy

Updated October 21, 2026. Applies to the native Acute Web app by CORE, maintained
by Iian Kehn. It does not describe third-party websites or the Android app.

## Data collection

Acute does not operate a service that receives your browsing activity. It has
no analytics, advertising SDKs, telemetry, or automatic diagnostic uploads.
It does not create an Acute account, sell personal data, or build advertising
profiles. No third-party runtime SDKs are bundled.
Acute does not request App Tracking Transparency authorization because it does
not perform cross-app or cross-website tracking of people.

## Data saved locally

Bookmarks (including folder names), start-page favorites, settings and
per-site preferences for regular tabs are stored in the app's local storage.
Site Settings in Settings lists every site with a saved preference. Browsing history is off by default; enabling it saves regular-tab
page titles, addresses and visit times locally. Turning it off does not delete
previously saved history. Regular-tab recovery is enabled by default and saves
web addresses, tab selection and page-view preferences for each window until you
disable it or replace the saved session. Saved addresses are complete, so they
can include anything a website puts in the address, such as search terms or
one-time sign-in codes. Closed iPad windows can leave a saved session behind;
turning off Restore regular tabs removes the saved sessions of every window. Recent-session sites and regular-tab previews are
held in memory. Regular websites can store cookies, caches and other website
data through WebKit. Acute asks before saving a download, and only for the tab you are viewing.
Downloads are saved in Acute's Documents/Downloads folder and are visible in
the Files app, including files downloaded in private tabs. A download that
stops can keep its partial file until you retry or remove it.

Acute reads modification times of files in its own Downloads folder to restore
the download list in date order. It uses app-local preferences for saved browser
data; neither API is used to fingerprint you. Local data may be included in
operating-system device backups according to your settings. Acute does not
provide its own cloud sync or backup service.

## Private browsing and its limits

Private tabs share one non-persistent WebKit website data store, so a sign-in
in one private tab is available to other private tabs. They are excluded
from saved browsing history, regular-tab recovery, recent sites and closed-tab
recovery. Acute does not capture private-tab previews and covers private browsing
when the app becomes inactive. Closing the last private tab releases the private website data store and
forgets site settings you changed in private tabs; those settings are never
written to storage. Private pages are not restored after the app closes.
User names and passwords entered for website sign-in prompts in private tabs
are not kept after the request. Files you download,
share or export remain until you remove them. Private browsing does not hide your
IP address or activity from websites, your network provider or your employer,
and does not extend to another app you choose to open.

## Websites, search and external services

Opening a website connects your device to that site and its resources; those
services receive normal network information such as your IP address and any
information you submit. Submitting a search sends your query to the search
provider selected in Settings (DuckDuckGo by default). Acute does not send search
suggestion requests as you type; address suggestions come only from bookmarks,
favorites and history already on your device. On-device tracker rules block a limited list
of known resource hosts, not all tracking. WebKit and operating-system security
services may make connections according to Apple's settings and policies.
Websites, search providers, external apps and Apple apply their own policies.

Camera or microphone access is requested only when a website asks and its tab
is on screen; Acute offers Allow Once, Always Allow on This Site or Don't Allow,
alongside system permission enforcement. You can switch a site between Ask,
Allow and Block in Site Privacy, review saved choices in Site Settings, and
revoke app permission in system Settings. Website uploads and information you enter into websites go to
the website you choose, not to an Acute service.

## Retention and deletion

Delete bookmarks in Library; you can also export them to a file you choose.
Remove favorites from the start page. Clear saved history in Library or
Settings; turning off Save browsing history offers to clear it. Reset saved
site preferences in Site Settings. Disable
Restore regular tabs to remove the saved recovery session. Close private tabs
to end those sessions. Clear Regular Website Data in Settings removes regular
cookies, caches and website storage, but not bookmarks, history, downloads or
private tabs. Site Privacy can clear the current site's data. Open websites
may create new data after clearing. Delete downloaded files in Downloads; copies
you export or share must be removed separately. Deleting the app, rather than
offloading it, removes its local container; device backups and exported copies
are managed separately through the relevant service or system settings.

## Support and policy changes

For support or privacy questions, contact the maintainer through
[GitHub Issues](https://github.com/iiankehn/acute-web-ios/issues).
Issues are public: do not post browsing history, credentials or other private
information. GitHub handles information you choose to submit under its own
privacy policy. No support report is sent automatically. This policy is included
in the app for offline reading; the current version is available in the project's
public repository. Policy changes accompany changes to the app's data practices.
